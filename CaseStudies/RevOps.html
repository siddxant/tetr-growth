The problem

The operation had outgrown the people running it

Inbound volume was growing into the hundreds of thousands of records a year. Every function touching a prospective customer was working in its own silo, with its own spreadsheet, and moving each record forward by hand.

A single customer journey crossed four teams and ten time zones. A hand-off from one team to the next meant a message, a wait, and a manual update somewhere else. Nobody had a live picture of where any customer actually was.

Leadership could not forecast. Teams could not see each other's work. And every new hire was spent on keeping up with volume, not improving the process.

Shared working hours between any two teams were a few hours a day. Every manual hand-off waited for the next overlap.

Silos

Four versions of the truth

Each vertical tracked the same customer in its own tool. Status conflicts were discovered in meetings, not in the system.

Manual work

People as the workflow engine

Reminders, follow-ups, evaluations and document checks were all done by hand, by a team that grew linearly with volume.

No visibility

Forecasting from memory

Without real-time data, pipeline forecasts and capacity planning were based on last month's export and gut feel.

My role

Owned the product end to end: PRD, engineering, rollout

I mapped the complete customer journey, defined the platform that would run it, and took it from a requirements document through build and into daily use by every team.

01 · DEFINE

Journey mapping and PRD

  • Mapped every state a customer passes through, across all four functions, into one journey model
  • Wrote the PRD for a stage-based operational platform with role-based views
  • Defined which steps had to be automated versus kept human, and the evaluation gates in between
02 · BUILD

Engineering delivery

  • Ran delivery with engineering: scoping, sprint priorities, acceptance
  • Built the automated communication layer (email and messaging) triggered by state changes
  • Introduced AI-driven evaluation to score and rank inbound records before human review
03 · ROLL OUT

Adoption across time zones

  • Migrated every team off spreadsheets onto one shared record
  • Role-based rollout so each function saw only its own stages and data
  • Real-time dashboards and forecasting for leadership from day one

Impact on the workload

The platform became the workflow engine, instead of people

20+ FTEof manual work eliminated. The people stayed; their time moved from moving records to making decisions.

02 / STAGE-BASED PIPELINE

One shared record, moving through stages

Why it mattered: hand-offs stopped being messages between teams and became a state change in the system.

Context travels with the customer

Why it mattered: the next team never starts from zero. Approve is one click because everything needed is already on the row.

Why it mattered: this is where most of the 20+ FTE went. Reminders and follow-ups no longer needed a person.

Documents stored securely, shared on a need-to-know basis

Customer documents are stored encrypted on cloud infrastructure with role-based access. A reviewer sees only what their role requires, identity can be masked for anonymous review, and every view or download is logged.

Why it mattered: finance, reviewers and success could work the same file without email attachments or shared drives.

09 / AI-DRIVEN EVALUATION

First-pass evaluation at the scale of the inbound

With hundreds of thousands of inbound records, human first-pass review was the bottleneck. The platform scores each record against the criteria the team already used, ranks the queue and shows the reviewer why, so people spend their time on the records that need judgement.

  • Transparent signals, not a black box: every score shows its reasons
  • Reviewers confirm or override; overrides feed back into the criteria
  • Final decisions stay human by design

Four stages, every nudge sent by the system

Map the journey before the tool

Every screen above exists because a state in the journey needed it. Starting from the map, not the feature list, is what let four teams agree on one system.

Automate the hand-off, not the decision

Reminders, routing and first-pass ranking went to the system. Final decisions stayed with people, and that is why the teams trusted it.

Rollout is the product

Role-based views and a per-team migration were as much work as the build. Adoption across ten time zones came from meeting each team where it already worked.
